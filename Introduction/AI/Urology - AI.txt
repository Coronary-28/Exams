Which of the following best describes the scope of urology as a specialty?
A) Surgical specialty dealing with diseases of the urinary tract and male genital tract
B) Medical specialty limited to glomerulonephritis and interstitial nephritis
C) Specialty limited to female reproductive disorders
D) Specialty exclusively managing chronic kidney impairment
E) Specialty limited to prostate cancer screening
Correct Answer: A) Surgical specialty dealing with diseases of the urinary tract and male genital tract
Explanation: Urology is defined as the surgical specialty managing diseases of the urinary tract and male genital tract, using both medical and surgical management. Nephrology, in contrast, medically manages conditions such as chronic kidney impairment, glomerulonephritis, and interstitial nephritis.

Regarding the vertical position of the kidneys relative to the vertebral column, which statement is correct?
A) The left kidney is 1–2 cm below the right kidney
B) The right kidney extends from the 1st to the 3rd lumbar vertebrae, and is 1–2 cm lower than the left
C) Both kidneys are at the same vertebral level
D) The right kidney extends from the 12th thoracic to the 3rd lumbar vertebrae, and is 1–2 cm lower than the left
E) The left kidney is located entirely within the pelvis
Correct Answer: B) The right kidney extends from the 1st to the 3rd lumbar vertebrae, and is 1–2 cm lower than the left
Explanation: The right kidney lies 1–2 cm lower than the left due to displacement by the liver, extending from L1–L3, while the left kidney extends from T12–L3.
###
A patient with a ureteric stone is being counseled on likely sites of impaction. Which of the following is NOT a recognized site of ureteric constriction?
A) Pelviureteric junction (PUJ)
B) Vesicoureteric junction (VUJ)
C) Where the ureter crosses the iliac vessels
D) Mid-abdominal ureter at the level of the renal pelvis
Correct Answer: D) Mid-abdominal ureter at the level of the renal pelvis
Explanation: The three true anatomical constrictions of the ureter are the PUJ, the point where it crosses the iliac vessels, and the VUJ; these are the classic sites of stone impaction.
###
During a living-donor nephrectomy, the surgical team preferentially selects one kidney for procurement based on venous anatomy. Which kidney is usually chosen, and why?
A) Right kidney, because its vein is longer
B) Left kidney, because its vein is longer
C) Right kidney, because it has more arteries
D) Left kidney, because it is smaller
E) Either kidney, since vein length is identical bilaterally
Correct Answer: B) Left kidney, because its vein is longer
Explanation: The right renal vein is shorter than the left, making the left kidney easier to anastomose and therefore the preferred choice in donor nephrectomy. This is a key clinically relevant anatomical asymmetry between the two renal veins.
###
A 40-year-old man with acute left flank pain also reports pain radiating to his left testicle. Which of the following best explains this referred pain?
A) Direct extension of infection from kidney to testis
B) Lymphatic spread from kidney to testis
C) Shared venous drainage between kidney and testis
D) Coincidental unrelated testicular pathology
E) Common innervation of the renal pelvis and testicle via T11–T12
Correct Answer: E) Common innervation of the renal pelvis and testicle via T11–T12
Explanation: The renal pelvis and testicle share afferent innervation at the T11–T12 spinal levels, explaining why renal pain can be referred to the testis and vice versa. This differs from radiating pain, which spreads along a single dermatome.
###
A patient presents with colicky flank pain, no fever, and is observed constantly moving and rolling in bed unable to find a comfortable position. What is the most likely underlying cause?
A) Ureteric obstruction from a stone
B) Acute pyelonephritis
C) Localized peritonitis
D) Retrocecal appendicitis
E) Vesicovaginal fistula
Correct Answer: A) Ureteric obstruction from a stone
Explanation: Colicky, fluctuating pain with the "rolling sign" (patient unable to lie still) and absence of fever is characteristic of obstructive stone disease rather than infection. Infectious causes like pyelonephritis typically present with steady pain and fever.
###
A patient describes pain that starts in the flank and is felt continuously along the same dermatome down toward the groin, without appearing in an unrelated separate site. This pattern best describes which type of pain?
A) Referred pain
B) Radiating pain
C) Shifting pain
D) Somatic pain
E) Visceral peritoneal pain
Correct Answer: B) Radiating pain
Explanation: Radiating pain is continuous along the extent of the same dermatome, unlike referred pain, which appears in two separate sites sharing a nerve root without continuity between them. Shifting pain instead moves entirely from one region to another over time.
###
A patient reports that blood is noted only at the very beginning of urination and then the urine clears. Based on the timing of hematuria, where is the likely site of bleeding?
A) Kidney
B) Bladder
C) Urethra
D) Ureter
E) Upper urinary tract
Correct Answer: C) Urethra
Explanation: Initial hematuria, where blood appears only at the start of urination before clearing, localizes the bleeding source to the urethra. Total hematuria throughout the stream instead suggests a bladder or upper tract origin.
###
A 60-year-old man presents with gross hematuria and passage of worm-shaped (vermiform) clots. This finding most strongly suggests bleeding originating from which site?
A) Prostatic urethra
B) Bladder neck
C) Glomeruli
D) Upper urinary tract (kidney/ureter)
E) Vaginal source
Correct Answer: D) Upper urinary tract (kidney/ureter)
Explanation: Vermiform (worm-shaped) clots form as blood is molded within the narrow ureteric lumen, indicating an upper tract source. Clot presence in general signals a more significant degree of hematuria and higher likelihood of underlying pathology.
###
A 65-year-old man presents with painless gross hematuria. What is the most common cause of gross hematuria in patients over age 50?
A) Renal cell carcinoma
B) Urolithiasis
C) Glomerulonephritis
D) Benign prostatic hyperplasia
E) Bladder cancer
Correct Answer: E) Bladder cancer
Explanation: Bladder cancer is the most common cause of gross hematuria in patients over 50 years old and must be excluded, as hematuria in adults is considered a symptom of malignancy until proven otherwise. Renal cell carcinoma is an important but less common differential.
###
A patient reports waking up three times per night to urinate. How many nocturnal voids are considered clinically significant?
A) One or more
B) Two or more
C) Three or more
D) Four or more
E) Five or more
Correct Answer: B) Two or more
Explanation: Nocturia is defined as one or more rises from sleep to void, but it is only considered clinically significant when there are two or more episodes. Like frequency, it can result from increased urine output or decreased bladder capacity.
###
A patient reports needing to use his abdominal muscles specifically during the terminal part of urination to complete voiding. This finding is best classified as which type of lower urinary tract symptom?
A) Urgency
B) Straining
C) Frequency
D) Nocturia
E) Hematospermia
Correct Answer: B) Straining
Explanation: Straining refers to the use of abdominal muscles to aid urination, normally used only at the end of voiding, and is classified as an obstructive/voiding symptom. This differs from irritative/storage symptoms such as frequency and urgency.
###
A urologist is about to perform a complete physical examination on a male patient, including digital rectal examination (DRE). At what point in the examination sequence should the DRE be performed, and in what position?
A) First, in supine position
B) First, in left lateral decubitus position
C) Last, in left lateral decubitus with knee-chest position
D) Last, in supine lithotomy position
E) Midway, in standing position
Correct Answer: C) Last, in left lateral decubitus with knee-chest position
Explanation: DRE should be performed at the end of the physical examination, with the patient in the left lateral decubitus position with knees drawn to the chest. The examiner separates the buttocks to inspect the anus before gently inserting a lubricated finger to assess sphincter tone and palpate the prostate.
###
A patient with suspected renal colic requires imaging to confirm the diagnosis. Which imaging modality is considered the gold standard for detecting urinary stones, and which modality would instead be preferred for staging prostate cancer?
A) MRI for stones; CT without contrast for prostate staging
B) Scrotal ultrasound for stones; bone scan for prostate staging
C) CT with contrast for stones; DMSA for prostate staging
D) CT without contrast for stones; MRI for prostate staging
E) Renal ultrasound for stones; micturating cystourethrogram for prostate staging
Correct Answer: D) CT without contrast for stones; MRI for prostate staging
Explanation: Non-contrast CT is the gold standard for detecting urinary tract stones, while MRI is used for local staging of prostate cancer. Bone scan is reserved for detecting malignant prostate metastasis, not staging the primary tumor.
###
On scrotal examination of an infertile male, both testes are palpable within the scrotum, but the vas deferens cannot be palpated bilaterally. This finding should raise suspicion for which underlying condition?
A) Testicular torsion
B) Varicocele
C) Cystic fibrosis
D) Epididymo-orchitis
E) Disorders of sexual differentiation
Correct Answer: C) Cystic fibrosis
Explanation: Bilateral absence of the palpable vas deferens despite normally descended, palpable testes is classically associated with congenital bilateral absence of the vas deferens, seen in cystic fibrosis. This is distinct from undescended testis, where the scrotum itself appears asymmetric and underdeveloped on one side.
