A patient arrives at the emergency department with wheezing and a history of asthma. In the triage process, you need to categorize the patient and determine the initial position or intervention. Given the patient's condition, which of the following is the most appropriate triage category and initial action?
A) Green (Minimal) - Have the patient lie flat and provide bronchodilators at a later time.
B) Red (Immediate) - Place the patient in an upright position and administer bronchodilators immediately.
C) Black (Deceased) - No intervention required, prioritize other patients.
D) Yellow (Delayed) - Place the patient in a semi-reclining position and administer bronchodilators as soon as possible.
E) Not classified - Refer the patient directly to an outpatient clinic without triage.
Correct Answer: D) Yellow (Delayed) - Place the patient in a semi-reclining position and administer bronchodilators as soon as possible.
Explanation:
A wheezing asthmatic without signs of severe respiratory failure is not yet at ESI Level 1/Red, but still requires prompt care, placing them in the Yellow/urgent range. A semi-reclining position eases work of breathing, unlike lying flat (Green), which would worsen dyspnea. Red is reserved for patients in profound distress or actively dying.
###
A patient arrives at the emergency department with a capillary refill time greater than 3 seconds. In the triage process, which of the following is the most appropriate triage category and initial action?
A) Yellow (Delayed) - Place the patient in a semi-reclining position and monitor closely before further action.
B) Red (Immediate) - Assess the patient urgently and initiate immediate resuscitation and stabilization.
C) Green (Minimal) - Have the patient sit comfortably and provide non-urgent care at a later time.
D) Black (Deceased) - No immediate action required.
E) Not classified - Delay assessment until vital signs are formally documented.
Correct Answer: B) Red (Immediate) - Assess the patient urgently and initiate immediate resuscitation and stabilization.
Explanation:
A capillary refill greater than 3 seconds signals poor peripheral perfusion and possible shock, a time-critical finding requiring immediate resuscitation, consistent with ESI Level 1/Red. Yellow or Green categories underestimate the danger of impending hemodynamic collapse, while Black applies only to patients who are deceased, not those who are still salvageable.
###
What is the primary purpose of assigning a triage category to a patient?
A) To prioritize the patient's care based on the level of urgency
B) To establish the patient's demographic information for hospital records
C) To determine the patient's previous medical conditions before treatment begins
D) To evaluate the patient's insurance coverage prior to admission
E) To assign the patient to a specific physician based on specialty preference
Correct Answer: A) To prioritize the patient's care based on the level of urgency
Explanation:
Triage exists to sort patients by medical acuity so the sickest are seen first, reflecting the ED principle that care is not given on a first-come, first-served basis. Demographic data, insurance status, and physician assignment are administrative functions and play no role in the acuity-based sorting that defines triage.
###
A 60-year-old male is brought to the ED. On assessment, his respiratory rate is 34/min, capillary refill time is 4 seconds, and he is unable to follow simple commands. What is the most appropriate triage category for this patient?
A) Yellow area
B) Green area
C) Red area
D) Discharge with outpatient follow-up
E) Fast track
Correct Answer: C) Red area
Explanation:
RR > 30/min and capillary refill > 3 seconds are signs of shock, and combined with an altered ability to follow commands, this patient is critically unstable. Any patient in shock requires immediate resuscitation and is placed in the Red area regardless of the specific diagnosis.
###
A 33-year-old female with a known history of asthma presents with difficulty breathing and wheezing for 3 days. She is able to speak in full, understandable sentences but must pause periodically to catch her breath. She is not in cardiac or respiratory arrest. What is the most appropriate triage category?
A) Green area
B) Yellow area
C) Red area
D) Immediate CPR and resuscitation
E) Fast track discharge
Correct Answer: B) Yellow area
Explanation:
Because she can still speak in full sentences, she does not meet resuscitation criteria and is taken out of the Red area. However, she still has objective difficulty breathing and requires active management, so she cannot be classified as low-acuity — placing her in the Yellow area.

A) Yellow area
B) Green area
C) Discharge home with a rescue inhaler
D) Red area
E) Fast track
Correct Answer: D) Red area

###
According to the described signs of respiratory distress in a critically ill patient requiring an immediate response, all of the following are correct EXCEPT:
A) Use of accessory muscles is a warning sign
B) Tachypnea (very fast breathing) is a warning sign
C) A patient may progress to confusion or drowsiness due to respiratory muscle fatigue
D) CO2 retention leading to drowsiness is especially concerning in COPD patients
E) Bradypnea with fully normal mentation is the expected pattern of deterioration
Correct Answer: E) Bradypnea with fully normal mentation is the expected pattern of deterioration
Explanation:
The described pattern of deterioration is accessory muscle use → tachypnea → confusion/drowsiness from respiratory muscle fatigue and CO2 retention, which is particularly dangerous in COPD patients. A calm, fully alert patient with slow breathing does not reflect this warning pattern — drowsiness/confusion is the expected finding, not normal mentation.

A trauma patient arrives at the ED. Which of the following is NOT considered a high-risk mechanism of injury requiring trauma team activation?
A) Fall from twice the patient's height
B) Penetrating trauma proximal to the knee/elbow, with bleeding controlled
C) Crush injury
D) Pedestrian struck by a vehicle (RTA)
E) Isolated femur fracture
Correct Answer: E) Isolated femur fracture
Explanation:
Fall from twice the patient's height, penetrating trauma proximal to the knee/elbow (with bleeding controlled), crush injury, and pedestrian-vehicle collisions are all recognized high-risk mechanisms that mandate a high level of trauma concern regardless of the patient's initial appearance. An isolated femur fracture, while serious, is a specific injury rather than a high-risk mechanism criterion.
###
A patient with full decision-making capacity refuses a proposed treatment after being informed of the risks and benefits. Which ethical principle does the physician's obligation to respect this refusal reflect?
A) Beneficence
B) Autonomy
C) Non-maleficence
D) Justice
E) Paternalism
Correct Answer: B) Autonomy
Explanation:
Autonomy is the ethical principle granting a competent patient the right to accept or refuse medical treatment, even if that refusal may lead to a poor outcome. The physician must honor this right as long as the patient has intact decision-making capacity.

What is the correct sequence of steps in basic life support (BLS) upon encountering an unresponsive patient?
A) Call 911 → Tap and shout → Check for breathing → Chest compressions
B) Tap and shout → Call 911 → Check for breathing → Chest compressions
C) Check for breathing → Tap and shout → Chest compressions → Call 911
D) Chest compressions → Tap and shout → Call 911 → Check for breathing
E) Tap and shout → Check for breathing → Call 911 → Chest compressions
Correct Answer: B) Tap and shout → Call 911 → Check for breathing → Chest compressions
Explanation:
The correct BLS sequence begins with assessing responsiveness (tap and shout), then activating emergency services (call 911), followed by checking for breathing, and finally starting chest compressions if the patient is not breathing normally. Following this order ensures help is on the way before proceeding with further steps.
###
What is the primary purpose of performing high-quality, early CPR in a patient in cardiac arrest?
A) To restore normal heart rhythm directly
B) To prevent irreversible brain damage by maintaining perfusion
C) To avoid the need for defibrillation
D) To confirm the diagnosis of cardiac arrest
E) To reduce the need for advanced airway management
Correct Answer: B) To prevent irreversible brain damage by maintaining perfusion
Explanation:
The brain is extremely sensitive to oxygen deprivation. High-quality CPR maintains circulation and oxygen delivery to vital organs, most critically the brain, until spontaneous circulation returns or definitive treatment (such as defibrillation) can be administered. Delaying CPR risks irreversible neurological injury.
